The real stack is what an Indian resident holding US ETFs through the LRS route actually pays on top: the broker, the exchanges, the bank's FX spread, and the Income Tax Department.

What is not modelled

Sell the book, bring the money home, pay what is owed. The dashboard's headline equity is a gross number — it has not met a broker, a bank, or a tax return. This is the same waterfall python3 run_live.py withdraw prints, with the assumptions exposed so you can set them to your own.

!
There is no ₹1.5 lakh tax-free allowance on this book. The ₹1,25,000 exemption is section 112A — long-term gains on listed Indian equity and equity mutual funds only. ₹1,50,000 is the section 80C deduction limit, a different thing entirely. US ETFs are foreign, unlisted assets: short-term gains go at your slab and long-term at 12.5%, with no per-year exemption. The allowance selector above defaults to ₹0 for that reason. The one real route to zero tax is the basic exemption and the 87A rebate on total income — a test on everything you earn that year, not a capital-gains allowance. Set it deliberately, and have it confirmed.

How often you withdraw costs more than how much

The remittance fee is a flat rupee amount, so it is charged per trip, not per rupee. Taking the same annual total in twelve monthly instalments pays it twelve times. Same amount withdrawn, same settings.

What fund size does to the friction

Percentage returns do not care how much you funded — the book is a set of weights. The charges very much do: the remittance fee, the per-order brokerage and the stepped GST are flat rupee amounts, so a small book pays them out of a small base. Same strategy, same day, same settings above.

How the book is run

Signal & sizing
  • Universe — 15 ETFs and crypto pairs across equities, rates, commodities, FX, crypto.
  • Signal — mean of the sign of the 3/6/12-month return, clipped at zero (long-only).
  • Sizing — each position scaled by 20% / trailing 60-day EWMA vol, so crude oil and 7-10y Treasuries contribute comparable risk.
  • Caps — 25% per asset, gross capped at 1.0x. No leverage, no shorting.
  • Vol target — the book is rescaled so trailing realised vol tracks 12%, then the per-asset cap is re-applied.
Execution
  • Rebalance — monthly, on the first update of a new calendar month.
  • Threshold — gaps worth less than 0.5% of equity are not traded.
  • Costs — 5bps of notional per side, deducted from cash at the fill.
  • Marks — Yahoo daily closes, twice each weekday; the mark-to-market moves daily, the decisions do not.
  • Currency — the book trades in USD and is reported in rupees at the day's rate, so USDINR moves show up in the P&L.

Read this before believing any of it

  • The backtest Sharpe is ~0.9, not 3. A real but modest edge.
  • Lower return than SPY. The case for it is the drawdown: −19% vs −55%. It is a diversifier, not a replacement for equity beta.
  • The longest backtested drawdown ran 784 days. Over two years underwater. 2023 lost 8.6%, 2012 lost 9.4%.
  • 40% of months lose money. There is no monthly-profit version of this.
  • It is in-sample. Universe, dates and parameters were chosen with hindsight. Robustness checks reduce the chance of curve-fitting; they do not eliminate it. Expect live results meaningfully worse than backtest.
  • ETFs are a proxy. Real managed futures run futures — cheaper, deeper, different financing. 5bps is an assumption, not modelled slippage.
  • 2008 and 2022 carry the record. Strip those two years and the edge over 60/40 largely disappears.
